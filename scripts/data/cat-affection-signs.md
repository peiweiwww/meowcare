TITLE: How Cats Show Love: Headbutts, Slow Blinks, Purring, and Other Affection Signals
SOURCES:
CATEGORY: behavior
TAGS: body-language, affection, slow-blink, headbutt, purring, communication
---

People who don't live with cats sometimes claim cats aren't affectionate. People who do live with cats know that's nonsense — cats are deeply affectionate, they just speak a different language than dogs. Once you learn the signals, you'll see your cat telling you they love you constantly.

**The slow blink.** This is the big one, often called the cat kiss. Your cat looks at you, slowly closes their eyes, holds for a beat, and slowly opens them again. In cat language, closing your eyes around someone means you trust them completely — predators don't close their eyes around things that might hurt them. The slow blink is your cat saying "I feel safe with you." You can slow blink back, and many cats will respond. It's a real conversation.

**Headbutting (also called bunting).** When your cat presses or rubs their forehead, cheeks, or chin against you, they're depositing scent from glands in those spots. They're literally marking you as theirs — part of their family unit, part of their territory in the friendly sense. Headbutts are reserved for trusted humans and friendly cats. A cat who headbutts you is claiming you as one of their people.

**Purring.** Mostly purring means contentment, but it's a more complicated signal than most people realize. Cats also purr when they're stressed, in pain, or even dying — the vibrations have a self-soothing effect and may even promote healing (some research suggests purring frequencies help bone density). So purring + relaxed body language = happy. Purring + tense body, hiding, or other stress signs = self-soothing through something difficult. In context, though, the purr you get when your cat is curled up on your lap is genuine affection.

**The tail held high with a curl at the top** is the cat equivalent of a happy greeting. When your cat walks toward you with their tail straight up and a little hook at the tip, they're saying "hi, I'm happy to see you." Same goes for a tail slowly curling around your leg or arm — that's a hug.

**Showing the belly.** A cat who flops onto their back and exposes their belly is showing extreme trust. The belly is the most vulnerable part of a cat's body, and they only expose it when they feel completely safe. Important caveat though: this is usually NOT an invitation to rub the belly. Most cats who show their belly will grab and bite if you touch it — they're showing trust, not asking for a rub. Some cats genuinely love belly rubs, but they're the exception.

**Following you around.** Cats who trail you from room to room, hang out wherever you are, sit on your laptop, or sleep on your stuff are choosing to be close to you. This is huge for an animal that values autonomy as much as cats do. Sleeping on something you've used (your shirt, your bed, your office chair) is comfort-seeking — your scent is a reassurance.

**Bringing you "gifts."** If your cat brings you a toy, a sock, or — less pleasantly — a dead bug or mouse, they're sharing. The interpretations vary (some experts think it's teaching, others think it's offering, others think they're just bringing prey to a safe spot to enjoy it later), but it's never aggressive. Receive the gift with appropriate gratitude even if it's gross.

**Kneading.** Covered separately, but in short — when your cat makes biscuits on you, they've reverted to kitten-mode and that's a high-trust behavior.

**Grooming you.** A cat who licks your hand, your arm, your hair, or your face is treating you like a member of their family group. Allogrooming (mutual grooming) is what cats do with their closest companions. The licks are sandpaper-rough, but the meaning is sweet.

**Sleeping near or on you.** Sleep is when cats are most vulnerable. Choosing to sleep where you are — pressed against you, in your lap, at the foot of the bed — means they trust you to keep them safe while they're at their most defenseless. Cats who sleep on your chest specifically are usually the most bonded.

**Things that LOOK negative but might be affection:**
- **Biting gently while being petted** is sometimes called "love biting" — gentle teeth without pressure, often during grooming-type interactions. It's affectionate, though it can also tip into the "petting tolerance reached" territory, so read context.
- **Trilling and chirping** — that little half-purr, half-meow noise — is a friendly greeting cats use mostly with their kittens and trusted humans.
- **Loud meowing at you** is communication — most adult cats only meow at humans, not at other cats. They're talking to you specifically.

**The most important signal: relaxed body language.** A loose, soft body, ears at neutral position, eyes half-closed or slow-blinking, slight purring — that's a content, affectionate cat. The combination of these signals around you, repeatedly, day after day, is the steady proof that your cat loves you. They might not jump and wag and slobber like a dog, but the bond is real, and once you see the signals, you can't unsee them.

If your cat does the slow blink at you tonight, slow blink back. You're now having a conversation.
