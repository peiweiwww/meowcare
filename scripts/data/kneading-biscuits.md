TITLE: Why Cats Knead (Make Biscuits) and What It Means
SOURCES: https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-behavior-issues
CATEGORY: behavior
TAGS: kneading, biscuits, contentment, scent-marking, behavior
---

If your cat presses their paws rhythmically into a soft blanket, your lap, or your stomach — alternating left and right, sometimes drooling, often purring — they're "making biscuits," also known as kneading. It's one of the most universally loved cat behaviors, and it almost always means good things.

**Where the behavior comes from.** Kneading starts in kittenhood. Newborn kittens knead their mother's belly while nursing to stimulate milk flow. The behavior gets paired with feelings of warmth, safety, and being fed — which probably explains why it persists into adulthood as a comfort behavior. Adult cats knead when they're content, relaxed, and feeling safe. Some cats also drool while kneading, which is a remnant of the same nursing instinct.

**What it means when your cat kneads you.** It's a high compliment. They feel safe enough with you to revert to kitten-mode behavior. They're also probably leaving their scent on you (cats have scent glands in their paws), which is their way of marking you as part of their territory in a friendly way. Some cats only knead specific people they're closest to.

**Why some cats knead more than others.** Kittens who were weaned very young often knead more as adults — possibly because they didn't get enough nursing time and the behavior is partially compensating. Some breeds and individual personalities are just more knead-y than others. Cats kneading more during stressful periods (after a move, after a vet visit, when there's tension in the house) are self-soothing — it's a coping mechanism.

**The claws problem.** Many cats extend their claws while kneading, which is great if they're on a blanket and painful if they're on your bare skin. Solutions:
- Keep their claws trimmed (the very tip, every couple of weeks)
- Put a blanket between them and your skin
- Don't punish or push them away — that breaks the trust they're showing by kneading you in the first place. Just rearrange the situation so it doesn't hurt.
- If they really go to town on you and a blanket isn't enough, gently redirect them to a blanket they can knead next to you

**When kneading might mean something else.** Almost always it's a contentment behavior, but a few situations are worth noting:
- **Excessive kneading combined with sucking on fabric** can be a comfort behavior in cats who were weaned too early. It's not harmful but can become compulsive in some cases.
- **Kneading combined with anxiety signs** (flattened ears, tail thrashing, dilated pupils) probably means they're self-soothing through stress, not relaxed. Look at the bigger context.
- **Sudden changes** — a cat who never kneaded suddenly kneading constantly, or vice versa — is sometimes worth mentioning at a vet visit, just because behavior changes in cats can be early signals of medical issues.

**Bottom line:** mostly just enjoy it. Your cat is paying you the highest possible compliment in cat language, which is essentially "you are my mom and this couch is my mom and everything is good." Try to take the pinpricks gracefully.
